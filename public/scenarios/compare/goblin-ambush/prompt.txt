A STRICTLY OVERHEAD, bird's-eye BATTLE MAP for a tabletop roleplaying game: every object is drawn exactly as seen from directly above with an orthographic camera pointing straight down (like a landscape plan painted in detail). No perspective, no side views, no isometric angle, no 3D elevation: trees are shown only as round leafy crowns seen from above with soft shadows beside them, bushes and thickets as flat clumps of foliage, boulders as flat grey rounded stones, and earth embankments as flat bands of bare brown earth and exposed roots with a dark shaded edge. Landscape 3:2 picture of a wooded roadside on a 30 by 20 square layout, in daylight. A wide worn dirt trail with scattered cobbles and small stones winds across the whole picture from the middle of the west (left) edge, gently curving, to the east (right) edge slightly lower down; it is about three squares wide. Two steep earth embankments run beside the trail, one above it on the north side and one below it on the south side, each a long curved band of bare earth and roots with a shaded drop, with gaps where the trail can be crossed. Dense thickets of bushes grow along the foot of both embankments, big enough to hide a creature, two thickets on each side of the trail about two thirds of the way east. Scattered grey boulders, a fallen log, tufts of tall grass and patches of mud. Large round tree crowns fill the four corners and the outer edges, leaving the middle open grass and trail. Meadow grass everywhere else. No grid lines, no text, no letters, no people, no animals, no tokens.
Art style: classic hand-drawn fantasy role-playing module cartography, as in the best published adventure maps: fine confident black ink linework with cross-hatching and stippled shading, warm sepia and parchment colour palette with soft watercolour washes, carefully hand-inked stone blocks and wood grain, subtle paper texture, gentle drop shadows under walls and objects, rich but restrained earth tones with a few warm accents, crisp and readable at tabletop scale.
