# Open World

OPEN WORLD is a sandbox campaign for a party of 1st-level characters, written to be improvised. It has a starting scene, a handful of live threads that lead into one another, a map of rumours and places, and a few rules for how the Dungeon Master should keep it open. Nothing here is a fixed plot: follow the players, let one thing lead to another, and write down everything that matters in the journal.

## THE SETTING: THE SUNDER MARCHES

A hundred years ago the kingdom of Aurelen broke apart on the night the Hollow Star fell into Hollowmere, a lake in the middle of the country. The sky there still bruises at dusk. The old royal road, the Lantern Road, is the only safe way across the Marches: nine hundred miles of cobbled track marked every mile by a cast-iron post lamp (a "marker lantern") that burns a pale blue flame by an old ward that keeps the Hush away from the road. The Hush is the name people give to the creeping stillness, the wrong dusk and the whispering things that come out of the marsh around Hollowmere. Travellers say that lanterns do not just give light, they hold the road together.

The road is run by the **Lanternwrights**, a guild of lamplighters and wardens who keep the flames lit, and who are tired, underpaid and stretched thin. Around the road: farm villages, toll bridges, fen-stilt hamlets, a handful of barons and abbots, ruins from the old kingdom, and a lot of people who want to be somewhere else by nightfall.

Tone: wayfarers' fantasy. Weathered, a little eerie, warm in the villages and cold between them. Humour is welcome; so is dread. Magic is real, common people are wary of it. No powers from published campaign settings; everything here is original to this table.

## HOW TO RUN IT (for the Dungeon Master)
- The party starts on the road with a reason to be together and a job. Everything after that is their choice. Offer, never force.
- Each session, make the world move: pick one thread below, advance its clock a step off-screen, and let the party find the traces. If they ignore a thread, it gets worse and the world remembers.
- Say yes to reasonable ideas, set a fair DC, and let failures complicate rather than stop things. Ask what the players want their characters to do, one short question at a time.
- When the party goes somewhere the table has no map for, narrate it without a map and add a journal entry of category place, status open, with the title "MAP WANTED: <name>" and a text of a few lines (size in squares, rooms or terrain, lighting, doors, hazards, mood). The player can then ask for the map to be made, and the table will have it next time.
- When you invent a named person, add a journal entry of category npc (name, look, voice tag, what they want, what they are hiding). Give each NPC a want, a fear and a secret. Reuse them: the world is small.
- Use the maps the table has (see the map list), and reuse them for other places when it fits (a camp, a roadside trail, an old cellar, a crypt). Say what the place is in the story even if the picture is generic.
- Fights are for a reason. Level 1 parties should face two to four weak foes, or a clever single enemy, and always have a way to retreat, parley or hide.

## OPENING SCENE
Dusk at Marker Nine of the Lantern Road, a day and a half east of the coast. The party are the hired guards of the wagon of **Maera Quillfeather**, a loud, cheerful halfling caravan master whose family firm hauls "lantern oil" (in truth: crates of star-glass shards, the one thing that can relight the ward-flames, being taken secretly to the guild's depot at Wickerby Crossing). Maera is camped at the roadside. As the party eat, the marker lantern beside the camp, which has burned pale blue for as long as anyone remembers, gutters, flares green and goes out. The air goes quiet. Somewhere in the dark a bell rings once, from the direction of nothing. That is the first time in a lifetime that a marker lantern has failed within sight of a camp.

Beginning the story: open with the camp, Maera, the dead lantern and the silence, and ask what the party does.

## LIVE THREADS (each leads to the next)
1. **The Dark Lanterns.** Marker lanterns are going out along the road, one every few nights, moving east. Clock: when the count reaches nine, a stretch of road is unwarded and the Hush floods it. Clue: every failed lantern has a faint hand-drawn spiral scratched into its post. Leads to the Ashen Choir and to Brother Ostin.
2. **The Missing Warden.** Captain Dessa Vane of the Lanternwrights is late reporting from the stretch west of Marker Nine. Her horse is found grazing, saddle empty. In the saddlebag: a half-burnt map fragment of "the chapel under the tollhouse" and a lantern key. Leads to the Old Tollhouse cellars and the Sunken Chapel.
3. **Baron Dray's Taxes.** Baron Corvane Dray has been squeezing the road tolls and has quietly hired sell-swords to "protect" the lamps (really, to seize the guild's depot). His tax collector's cart is overdue at Wickerby. Leads to a bandit trail ambush and to a negotiation with the baron.
4. **The Fen-kin Song.** The marsh people, the Fen-kin, hear a song from Hollowmere that is getting louder; their elder, Sera Reedwhisper, wants outsiders to carry a message to the guild, because the fen-kin are not trusted. Leads to Hollowmere itself.
5. **Star-glass.** The shards in Maera's crates are being hunted. Somebody in the party's own camp already knows what is in them.

## THE ASHEN CHOIR (the hidden hand)
A small cult of ex-lamplighters, scholars and the grief-stricken who believe the Hollow Star is calling and that the wards are a cage. Their Cantor (name: **Ilthe Marrow**, a soft-voiced former guild chorister) is not evil so much as certain. Choir members mark the lanterns they put out with a spiral and leave a single white petal. They want the star-glass to open the star "gently". They are wrong about what is inside Hollowmere (a prisoner bound to the star, not a god), and the truth is the campaign's hidden turn, to be revealed only when the party earns it.

## PEOPLE
- **Maera Quillfeather** (halfling, caravan master, loud, kind, afraid of the dark): wants her shipment delivered; hides that she knows what the crates hold. Voice: sly or feminine.
- **Brother Ostin** (human, lantern-priest, doubting): lights the guild shrine lamps at Wickerby; wants forgiveness; knows the spiral mark from an old hymn book. Voice: elderly.
- **Captain Dessa Vane** (half-orc, Lanternwright warden, gruff): missing; wants the road safe; is alive, held by the Choir, and unwilling to name a traitor. Voice: gruff.
- **Pip Tallowick** (human child, runner and lamp-boy at Wickerby Crossing): sees everything, trusts nobody, can be bought with sweets. Voice: child.
- **Baron Corvane Dray** (human noble, silver-tongued): wants the road, not the world burned; will pay the party well for small favours that hurt others. Voice: noble.
- **Sera Reedwhisper** (Fen-kin elder, a marsh-folk of reed and heron feathers): wants to be heard. Voice: ethereal or elderly.
- **Ilthe Marrow** (the Cantor): speaks gently, never raises a hand, never lies outright. Voice: ethereal.

## PLACES (reuse the map list for these, or note a MAP WANTED)
- **Marker Nine camp** (camp maps): the party's starting ground.
- **Wickerby Crossing** (MAP WANTED: a frontier village at a river ford with a Lanternwright depot, a shrine, an inn called the Drowned Lamp, a mill and a toll bridge): the first hub.
- **The Thornwood trail** (roadside ambush map): a bend of the road under dark trees where Dray's missing tax cart is found.
- **The Old Tollhouse cellars** (cellars map): abandoned tollhouse with storerooms and a hidden stair down.
- **The Sunken Chapel** (crypt maps): a crypt chapel of the old kingdom below the tollhouse, where the first marker lantern was lit and where a Choir meeting is held.
- **Hollowmere** (MAP WANTED: black still water, a reed-edged shore, a sunken tower, the pale star shape deep below): the end of the road, to reach only after a long road of choices.

## ENCOUNTERS ON THE LANTERN ROAD (roll d8, or choose)
1 A pair of wolves driven out of the fens (use wolves). 2 A toll-bandit trio (bandits) who will take a bribe. 3 A lost pilgrim, actually a Choir scout (a scout). 4 A wreck of an overturned wagon and a frightened child (nothing hostile, a moral choice). 5 A pack of giant rats in a tollhouse cellar. 6 The Hush: a stretch of road goes quiet, sound dies, and shadows stand a pace too long (a sanity-style scene; shadows if the party lingers). 7 A trader with a cart of oddities (a shop; magic items from the SRD at fair prices). 8 Fen-kin hunters, wary but curious.

## REWARDS AND GROWTH
Milestone and experience both work. The party levels up after resolving a thread or two. Treasure is small and strange: star-glass shards (they hum when a lantern is near), guild lamp keys, marsh charms, a signet of the Baron. Do not hand out powerful magic early.
